How this calculator works

RPM is revenue after YouTube's share per 1,000 views. For videos, it includes all views, not only monetized playbacks; for Shorts, it uses engaged views. RPM can include ads, YouTube Premium, memberships and fan funding. See YouTube's RPM definition.

This tool multiplies long-form views by an assumed niche rate and country multiplier, then adds Shorts engaged views at assumed rates of $0.05, $0.10 and $0.15 per 1,000 for the low, midpoint and high scenarios. Country multipliers apply only to long-form views. The niche rates and multipliers are illustrative inputs with no verified dataset supplied. The midpoint is an arithmetic average, not a typical or expected outcome. Annual results assume the same monthly inputs for 12 months. No additional platform-share deduction is applied.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a fixed YouTube rate per 1,000 views?
No. Actual RPM varies by channel and revenue mix. This calculator uses illustrative assumptions; use your own YouTube Analytics to assess actual performance.
How many views do I need to make $1,000 a month?
For long-form video, divide $1,000 by your assumed RPM and multiply by 1,000. At an illustrative $20 RPM, that is 50,000 views; at $2.75, round up to 363,637 views. These examples are arithmetic, not income promises.
Do YouTube Shorts pay less than regular videos?
This model assumes lower Shorts rates, but does not establish a universal payout ratio. Shorts RPM uses engaged views. Enter that metric from YouTube Analytics rather than total Shorts starts or replays.
Is this estimate exact?
No. The rates are illustrative assumptions without a documented source dataset. The range is a scenario range, not a statistical confidence interval, and actual earnings may fall outside it.
Does YouTube pay monthly?
Yes. Once you are in the YouTube Partner Program and cross the $100 threshold, AdSense pays out monthly, usually between the 21st and 26th.
