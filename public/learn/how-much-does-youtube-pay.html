How Much Does YouTube Pay? Illustrative Earnings Math

By PlayMETRIC Team  •  Published July 21, 2026  •  Updated October 23, 2026

YouTube does not pay a fixed amount per view. RPM depends on the channel, audience, format, period and revenue sources. Numbers below are illustrative assumptions, not verified 2026 earnings data.

TL;DR
  • The lower scenario assumes $0.50 to $6 RPM, equivalent to $0.0005 to $0.006 per view; it is not a measured typical range.
  • The higher scenario assumes $10 to $30 RPM to illustrate the effect of changing the input.
  • Shorts use a separate revenue-sharing model and RPM based on engaged views. There is no universal payout per 1,000 views or fixed ratio to long-form earnings. Use your own YouTube Analytics data; calculator amounts are illustrative assumptions.
  • Watch Page Ads generally pay creators 55% of net ad revenue; Shorts pay 45% of the allocated Creator Pool revenue. These are different bases.
  • Subscribers pay nothing directly. Views, niche, and audience country decide your income.

How the payment system actually works

  1. Join the YouTube Partner Program: you need 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days.
  2. Ads run on your videos: advertisers bid in an auction for your viewers. Their bids set your CPM.
  3. Revenue sharing differs: Watch Page Ads pay creators 55% of net ad revenue. Shorts pay creators 45% of their allocated Creator Pool revenue.
  4. YouTube pays monthly: once your balance passes $100, payment arrives between roughly the 21st and 26th of the following month.

What about pay per subscriber?

There is no fixed payout per subscriber. Revenue depends on monetization activity; subscriber count alone is not an earnings forecast.

How Shorts earnings differ

Shorts use a separate revenue-sharing model and RPM based on engaged views. There is no universal payout per 1,000 views or fixed ratio to long-form earnings. Use your own YouTube Analytics data; calculator amounts are illustrative assumptions.

Frequently asked questions

How much does YouTube pay for 1 million views?
At assumed RPMs of $0.50 and $6, one million video views yields $500 and $6,000 respectively. Actual earnings require your observed RPM; these are examples, not typical payouts.
How many views do I need to earn $1,000 a month?
At an assumed $3 RPM, about 333,334 monthly views; at an assumed $18 RPM, about 55,556. These are arithmetic examples, not forecasts for a niche.
Does YouTube pay more for longer videos?
Videos over 8 minutes can include mid-roll ads, which meaningfully raises revenue per view. Past that, watch time helps indirectly by improving distribution.
When does YouTube pay out?
Monthly through YouTube's creator payment system, usually between the 21st and 26th, once your balance reaches the $100 minimum.
How much does YouTube pay per subscriber?
Nothing directly. Subscribers do not generate revenue on their own. They matter because they reliably supply views, and views carry the money.
How much do YouTube Shorts pay compared to long form?
Shorts use a separate revenue-sharing model and RPM based on engaged views. There is no universal payout per 1,000 views or fixed ratio to long-form earnings. Use your own YouTube Analytics data; calculator amounts are illustrative assumptions.
